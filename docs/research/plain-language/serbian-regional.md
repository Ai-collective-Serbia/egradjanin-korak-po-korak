# Plain, easy-to-read and controlled language for Serbian and neighbouring languages

Research date: 2026-10-07. Web research only. Every claim below has a primary URL unless marked
"secondary" or "not verified". Tradition tags: **T1** = controlled language (rules plus approved
dictionary), **T2** = plain language (ISO 24495-1, government clear-writing guides),
**T3** = easy-to-read for people with intellectual disabilities.

## 1. Summary

1. **No controlled language (T1) exists for Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian, Montenegrin or Slovenian.** No ASD-STE100-style rule set or one-meaning dictionary was found in any script.
2. **No source in the region ships an approved word list.** The nearest raw material is the Serbian frequency dictionary (Kostić, first version 1999; secondary source only).
3. **Serbia has not adopted ISO 24495-1 as SRPS.** The ISS catalogue page shows only the ISO original. Slovenia adopted it as SIST ISO 24495-1:2024 ("Preprost jezik"), in English and French only. No Croatian, Bosnian or Montenegrin adoption was found.
4. **The most usable Serbian source is a T3 one.** Inclusion Europe's "Информације за све / Informacije za sve" exists in Serbian Cyrillic and Latin, adapted by Caritas Srbije around 2023. It has 45 written-text rules, five of them specific to Serbian.
5. **The best-developed regional rule set is Slovenian (T3).** "Lahko je brati 2: Pravila" (Zavod Risa, 2019) has 59 numbered rules, including "/" is hard, write "ali" instead.
6. **The best government T2 text in the region is Croatian.** The "Standard razvoja javnih e-usluga" (2021) asks for short sentences, closed questions and a "Nisam siguran/na" answer option.
7. **Serbia's official web rules are thin.** ITE "Смернице в.5.0" (2014) only says texts should be written by trained people and proofread. No eUprava content style guide was found.
8. **No readability formula has been calibrated for Serbian.** Croatian has SMOG-Cro (Brangan, 2011 thesis, 2015 article) and a Flesch variant (Brangan, 2014).
9. **The easy-to-read standard and Pravopis conflict in two places.** The first is date format: "13.10.2023." against "13. 10. 2023.". The second is digits against words for small numbers.
10. **Gender forms are legally unsettled.** The Constitutional Court suspended the 2021 gender equality law in June 2024 and has issued no final ruling that we found. The Serbian standardisation board, in its 2011 decision no. 60, advises against "-о/-ла" slash forms.

## 3. The most usable sources, rule by rule

### 3.1 Информације за све / Informacije za sve, Serbian adaptation (T3, Caritas Srbije, about 2023)

The standard has three parts: 20 general rules, 45 rules for written text and 26 rules for websites. All quotes below are from the Latin PDF.

**Words**
- Rule 6: "Koristite lako razumljive reči, koje ljudi dobro poznaju."
- Rule 7: avoid complicated words. If one is needed, "objasnite ih na jasan način".
- Rule 9: "Koristite istu reč da opišete isti pojam u celom dokumentu." This is the one-term-one-meaning principle, without a dictionary.
- Rule 10: no metaphors. The example given is "Pao mi je kamen sa srca".
- Rule 11: no foreign words unless well known.
- Rule 12: "Izbegavajte korišćenje inicijala." If you must use one, explain it, for example "EU" means "Evropska unija".
- Written rule 12: be careful with pronouns. Use the name if it is unclear who is meant.
- Written rules 15 and 16: avoid special characters "\, &, <, § ili #" and abbreviations such as "npr." and "itd.".

**Sentences**
- Rule 14: short sentences.
- Rule 15: address people directly with "ti" or "vi".
- Rule 16: positive sentences, not negative ones.
- Rule 17: active, not passive. The example is "Doktor će vam poslati pismo".
- Written rule 17: every new sentence starts on a new line.
- Written rule 18: never hyphenate a word across two lines.
- Written rule 19: "napišite samo 1 misao u svakoj rečenici". When possible, one sentence fits on one line. If it must wrap, break it where people would pause when reading aloud.

**Numbers**
- Rule 13: percentages and large numbers are hard. Use "malo" or "mnogo" instead.
- Serbian-specific rules 41 to 45:
  - 41: avoid ordinals such as "7. sastanak".
  - 42: write numbers as digits, "1, 2, a ne jedan, dva", and never use Roman numerals.
  - 43: prefer the present tense over the past tense.
  - 44: no elisions such as "k'o" or "je l'".
  - 45: "pišite datume brojevima. Na primer: utorak, 13.10.2023. Nemojte pisati 13. oktobar 2023."

**Layout**
- At least Arial 14. Sans-serif type only, no italics, no condensed or light fonts.
- Never write whole words in capitals. Use one font only.
- Underline only links. Do not rely on colour.
- Align text left, never justified, and do not write in columns.
- Leave space between paragraphs. Do not indent first lines.
- Use real bullet lists, not comma lists, but not too many subheadings or bullets.
- Put pictures next to the text, and use the same picture for the same concept.

**Web**
- Users can return home in one click. The navigation bar has 7 or 8 items at most and stays in the same place.
- Use large clear buttons for next, previous and home. Avoid pop-ups and animations, and avoid horizontal scrolling.
- Long URLs are hidden behind simple words. Links are words, not images.
- Test with people with intellectual disabilities. This is web rule 1 and general rule 5.

### 3.2 Lahko je brati 2: Pravila (T3, Zavod Risa, Slovenia, 2019)

The book has 59 numbered rules and is itself written in easy-read style. These are the rules most relevant to us.

- **Rule 3, lower case:** use lower case, because capitals "na spletu pomenijo kričanje". This reverses the Sožitje 2012 rule of writing everything in capitals.
- **Rules 7 to 14, words:**
  - 7: use native words instead of foreign ones.
  - 8: use easier words.
  - 9: use the same words throughout.
  - 10: be careful with acronyms.
  - 11: use no abbreviations.
  - 12: be careful with pronouns.
  - 13: be careful with diminutives.
  - 14: colloquial words are allowed when they are easier.
- **Rules 15 to 21, sentences:**
  - 15: each clause goes on its own line.
  - 16: clauses are short. Split a sentence like "Sonja peče kruh, saj ..." into two sentences.
  - 17: never split words across lines.
  - 18: no metaphors.
  - 19: address people directly. The book uses formal "vi" for adults the reader does not know.
  - 20: prefer affirmative sentences, unless the negative form is the everyday one.
  - 21: active voice.
- **Rules 22 to 24, numbers:**
  - 22: numbers up to 10 may be digits or words; check which the readers understand better.
  - 23: big numbers are hard. "Slovenija ima 2.066.880 prebivalcev" becomes "več kot 2 milijona".
  - 24: percentages are hard. "znižali za 60 %" becomes "za več kot polovico".
- **Rule 38, special signs:**
  - The en dash for a range becomes "od ... do": "od 10.00 do 19.00".
  - "&" becomes "in".
  - **"Znak / pomeni ali. Raje uporabimo besedo ali."** The example is "toplo/hladno" becoming "toplo ali hladno".
  - Quotation marks are dropped and speech is set in bold.
- **Rules 25 to 37, text and layout:**
  - Know the readers, add needed information and keep the text short.
  - The title must be clear. Pick only the important information.
  - Align left and tell things in order.
  - Put each list item on its own line and leave white space.
  - Be careful with columns.
  - Use more space between lines and between paragraphs.
  - Number the pages.
- **Rules 39 to 49, notes, tables and pictures:** no footnotes; explain tables and graphs; use a plain background; use pictures beside the text, check them with users and keep them consistent.
- **Rules 50 to 59, labelling:** label easy-read text. There is a Slovenian easy-read logo besides the European one. Four difficulty levels are defined, from sensory reading to level four.

### 3.3 Standard razvoja javnih e-usluga u RH: Smjernice (T2, Croatia, 2021)

This is a government standard written for exactly our kind of product: step-by-step public e-services.

- **Section 2.4, service names:**
  - Put the outcome in the service name, for example "Prijava vjenčanja".
  - "Koristite glagole umjesto imenice."
  - "Jedna usluga jedan naziv."
  - Use standard-language terms "koje sami korisnici svakodnevno koriste dok pretražuju usluge".
- **Section 2.5.1, forms:** split a form so that each page has one type of question, one topic and one decision. Use branching questions. Start with questions that tell users whether they qualify at all.
- **Section 2.5.2, consistency:** "Sučelje treba koristiti nedvosmislene riječi i jednostavne opise." Use the same structure on every screen. Plain wording makes the service inclusive "za ljude koji imaju poteškoća s čitanjem".
- **Section 2.5.6, "Sažet i izravan jezik korisničkog sučelja":**
  - Users scan rather than read.
  - "Preporuča se uporaba kratkih rečenica i izbacivanje svih nepotrebnih riječi."
  - Do not refer to shape, size, colour or location. Avoid "Kliknite na zeleni gumb" and "Koristite izbornik s lijeve strane".
  - Prefer "niz jednostavnih pitanja zatvorenog tipa" to one complex open question.
  - "preporučljivo je omogućiti korisnicima da odgovore s „Nisam siguran/na" ili „Ne znam"".
  - Offer help text that explains where to find the answer, what each choice leads to, and any legal jargon.
- **Section 2.5.4, contrast:** text and controls must meet WCAG 2.1 level A contrast.

### 3.4 ITE Смернице в.5.0 (T2, weak, Serbia, 2014)

This is the only official Serbian guidance on web content language. It is still the current version: no v6 or later was found.

- **Section 3.3:** state body websites "мора да буду на српском језику и на ћириличком писму". "Обавеза је да се сав садржај презентације уноси на ћириличном алфабету, а да се омогући транслитерација". The stated reason is to avoid foreign words written in the original script. This matches our "author in Cyrillic, generate Latin" build.
- **Section 3.7.2, "Лакоћа читања и логичког организовања садржаја":** texts for people with comprehension difficulties should be written or corrected by "особе које су обучене да јасно представе информације", and all texts should pass "лекторску супервизију". There are no sentence or word rules.
- **Section 3.7.2, "Графичка алтернатива садржаја":** important procedures should also be shown as illustrated narratives, infographics, photo sequences or video.
- **Sections 3.5 and 3.7:** use a top-down hierarchical structure and follow WCAG 2.0. The section also warns that reCAPTCHA is inaccessible to blind users.

## 4. Documented negatives

These are things searched for and not found, with the evidence for each.

- **SRPS ISO 24495-1, 24495-2 or ISO/IEC 23859.** The ISS project pages for ISO 24495-1 and 24495-2 show no SRPS line. "SRPS ISO 24495" returned nothing, "24495" restricted to iss.rs returned nothing, and the ISS search URL returned 404.
- **HRN, MEST or BAS adoptions of ISO 24495-1.** Exact-designation searches returned nothing. One HZN e-glasilo (11/2025), one SIST monthly list (2024-05) and one ISME list were grepped with no hit. Only SIST adopted it, found through iTeh.
- **"Informacije za sve" in Bosnian, Montenegrin or Macedonian.** Inclusion Europe's language list has none, and a search for Montenegrin or Bosnian copies returned nothing.
- **ITE guidelines v6 or later, and any eUprava content style guide.** Neither was found. The 2014 v5.0 is the latest listed on arhiva.ite.gov.rs.
- **A Serbian "jasan jezik" or "razumljiv jezik" guide** from the government, the Zaštitnik građana, the Poverenik, universities or NGOs. Searches in Latin and Cyrillic found only the 2019 public-administration-reform framework naming the problem and the data protection law's "јасних и једноставних речи" clause.
- **A Serbian easy-read rule set of Serbian origin.** MDRI-S, Naša kuća and VelikiMali produced "Pravo da donesem odluku" (May 2010), which is a policy report, not easy-read. The only Serbian easy-read rules found are the Caritas adaptation of the Inclusion Europe standard.
- **Croatian statutory plain-language duty.** The Zakon o hrvatskom jeziku (NN 14/24) has no clarity or plain-language article. No Croatian government "jasan jezik" handbook was found. The IHJJ ran a 2024 project "Muško i žensko u hrvatskome jeziku" and a "Jasan hrvatski prijevod" workshop, both secondary sources with no guide found.
- **A Croatian rule set from Hrvatski savez udruga osoba s intelektualnim teškoćama.** None was found. OSIOZG reportedly has an easy-language handbook, but it was not located.
- **Slovenian "jasen jezik" government standard.** Only SIST ISO 24495-1:2024 was found. A "Priročnik o jeziku in obliki dopisa" was mentioned in search results but not verified.
- **Controlled language for any of these languages (T1).** Searches in Latin and Cyrillic found no STE-like project or one-meaning dictionary.
- **A readability formula calibrated for Serbian.** Croatian formulas exist. Serbian work applies LIX without calibration. "Readability of Serbian Law on Auditing" on ResearchGate was not read.
- **A final Constitutional Court ruling on the 2021 gender equality law.** None was found as of 2026-10-07.

## 5. Pravopis constraints

The edition read is Правопис српскога језика, измењено и допуњено издање, Матица српска 2010, edited by Pižurica, Dešić, Ostojić and Stanojčić. The paragraph numbers ("т.") below come from that edition. Later reprints exist: MEI cites a 2015 printing, and web sources mention 2017 and 2022. The text was read from a school-hosted scan at https://osvalter.edu.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Pravopis.pdf.

- **Capitals for institutions, т. 63.** Only the first word of an institution's name takes a capital, plus any word that is a proper name by itself. Examples: "Министарство спољних послова Републике Србије", "Универзитет у Београду". The same note says "интернет" is written in lower case. So write "Министарство унутрашњих послова" and "Пошта". Do not capitalise every word.
- **Acronyms, т. 181.** Acronyms are written in capitals without dots or spaces. Case endings follow a hyphen in lower case: "из ПТТ-а", "са ММФ-ом", "на CD-у". So write "ЈМБГ-а" and "ЈМБГ-ом". Pravopis itself says acronyms are "прилично оптерећење за разумевање текста од стране шире читалачке публике". This supports the easy-read rule to explain or avoid them. Foreign acronyms may stay in the original Latin, for example CD or PC, or be transliterated if widely known.
- **Foreign names and brands, т. 195 and the т. 80s.**
  - Adapted writing is "опште правило ћириличких правописа". Original spelling applies in Latin.
  - Original Latin inside Cyrillic text is "само изузетан поступак".
  - Names of firms and products "(у рекламама на пример)" may keep their source spelling.
  - So "Гугл" in Cyrillic, generated as "Google" in Latin, is Pravopis-compliant.
  - Pravopis lists "имејл/мејл" with plural "имејлови", and "имејл-адреса". "e-mail" in Latin letters is only for addresses and business cards.
  - The dictionary part has "лозинка" and "кликнути" with aorist "кликнух", not "клићи".
- **Quotation marks, т. 168.** Quotation marks mark quoted text, irony and titles. Pravopis allows omitting them when the quotation is set apart typographically. That matches Slovenian rule 38, which sets speech in bold instead.
- **Dates and ordinals.** Pravopis practice, as applied in the MEI 2016 guidelines, writes "12. 2. 1992." with a space after each ordinal period. A full date takes "године": "1. јануара 1993. године". Four-digit numbers take no separator, so write "4000" and "3200", not "3.200". Numbers with five or more digits take a period, as in "12.000".
- **Time.** The MEI guidelines write "17.00", with a period. A Pravopis example in the comma section (т. 147–157) writes "у 12,30 часова", with a comma. Neither uses a colon. MEI prefers "17.00 сати" to "часова". The colon form "9:30" appears on popular advice sites (secondary) and is not supported by the Pravopis text read.

**Conflicts between easy-read rules and Pravopis.** A house style must choose, and should know what it overrides.
1. **Date spacing.** Easy-read rule 45 writes "13.10.2023." with no spaces. Pravopis practice is "13. 10. 2023.".
2. **Digits or words.** Easy-read rule 42 says always digits. Conventional prose style prefers words for small numbers. Slovenian rule 22 says to test which readers understand better.
3. **Slash.** Pravopis has no rule that bans "/". The Slovenian easy-read rules discourage it, and the Serbian standardisation board discourages the gender slash specifically (section 6).

## 6. Gender-form facts

These are facts only, with no recommendation.

- **The law.** Закон о родној равноправности, Сл. гласник РС 52/2021, primary text at http://www.parlament.gov.rs/upload/archive/files/cir/pdf/zakoni/2021/741-21.pdf.
  - чл. 6 т. 17 defines "родно осетљив језик" as language that promotes equality of women and men.
  - чл. 25 requires public bodies to monitor "употребу родно осетљивог језика у називима радних места, положаја, звања и занимања".
  - чл. 37 ст. 1 т. 4 подт. (3) covers gender-sensitive language "у складу са граматичким родом" in textbooks, certificates, diplomas, titles and licences.
  - чл. 44 ст. 3 requires the media to use it.
  - чл. 73 ст. 2 says these language duties apply three years after adoption, which is from June 2024.
- **The Constitutional Court.** At its session of 27 June 2024, in case IUz-85/2021, the court opened proceedings on the constitutionality of the whole law. It suspended the execution of individual acts and actions taken under the law until the case is decided. Sources are secondary: paragraf.rs (2024-09-02), propisi.net and slobodnaevropa.org. The court reasoned that the obligatory gender-sensitive language conflicts with the obligatory use of standard Serbian, and that the Constitution does not know "род". OSSJ's statement of 14 October 2024 dates the resolution 28 June 2024 (https://www.ossj.rs/odluke-i-saopstenja/povodom-resenja-ustavnog-suda-srbije-od-28-6-2024-godine/). No final decision was found as of 2026-10-07.
- **The Serbian standardisation board.** Одбор за стандардизацију српског језика, Одлука бр. 60 of 24 February 2011, drafted by Sofija Miloradović (secondary: nspm.rs, 2017): "Korišćenje paralelnih formi ili navođenje forme u muškom rodu sa oznakom za nastavak u ženskom rodu nepotrebno opterećuje rečenicu", and leads to absurdity. The board does not recommend forms like "урадио/ла". Its October 2024 statement repeats that only the standard language applies and criticises gender-sensitive usage.
- **Regional easy-read and plain-language guides.**
  - Inclusion Europe, all versions, does not address gender forms. Its special-character rule lists "\, &, <, § ili #", which does not include "/".
  - Slovenian Zavod Risa rule 38 says "/" means "ali" and should be replaced by the word. Its examples use the generic masculine: "V knjigi boš (ti) našel pravila".
  - The Croatian government e-services standard of 2021 itself recommends offering "Nisam siguran/na" and writes "riješio/la".
  - Serbian textbooks use several variants: "naučio/naučila", "naučio/-la" and "naučio/la" (secondary: sr.wikipedia "Rodno osetljiv jezik"; a ResearchGate paper on textbooks, not read).
