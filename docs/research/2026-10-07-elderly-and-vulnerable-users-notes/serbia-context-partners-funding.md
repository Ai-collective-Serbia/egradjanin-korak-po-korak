# Serbia and Western Balkans context for helping elderly and vulnerable people with eUprava / eID: adoption data, programmes, partners, funding

Researched 2026-10-07. Each source is labelled with its date and type: official, media, NGO/IO (international organisation) or aggregator. Every finding is tied to the "eGrađanin korak po korak" static wizard (Astro, GitHub Pages, no backend, Cyrillic content with generated Latin pages, a post-office counter card). Method note: two PDFs could not be read with the tools available here: the UNDP 2022-23 citizen survey on public administration digitalisation and the Caritas Serbia 2026 accessibility report. They are listed as gaps.

## 1. Internet, smartphone and e-government use by age, education and income (RZS, Eurostat)

### Takeaway
About 9 in 10 Serbians aged 16-74 use the internet: 90.0% in 2025, with 5.2% who have never used it. The 65-74 group is far behind: about 2 in 3 are online and about 1 in 5 have never used the internet. Those who are online use phones almost entirely, which supports the project's phone-first design. Only 33% of Serbians have basic digital skills, against an EU average of 56%. The official statistics do not cover anyone aged 75 or older, and no source gave e-government use by age for Serbia.

### Cited Findings
- RZS 2025 ICT survey of households and individuals aged 16-74, using Eurostat methodology. 90.1% of households have internet access (up 1.3 percentage points on 2024). 90.0% of individuals used the internet in the last 3 months and 5.2% have never used it. 71.7% of households own a computer, down 1.7 percentage points. — [RZS release, 2025-10-24, official](https://www.stat.gov.rs/sr-latn/vesti/20251024-godisnje-istrazivanje-o-ikt-2025/?a=27&s=0). The full publication is [G202516020.pdf](http://publikacije.stat.gov.rs/G2025/Pdf/G202516020.pdf). It is over 10 MB and could not be parsed here, so the age tables were not checked against the primary source.
- RZS 2024 survey: 87.7% used the internet and 7.3% had never used it. 88.8% of households had internet access. — [RZS release, 2024-10-25, official](https://www.stat.gov.rs/sr-latn/vesti/20241025-godisnje-istrazivanje-o-ikt-2024/?a=27&s=0)
- Age breakdown, credited to RZS 2025 (2,800 households, ages 16-74). This is a **secondary source**:
  - Ages 55-64: 81.2% used the internet in the last 3 months, 4.5% never have, 97.1% go online by mobile phone, 39.4% use internet banking.
  - Ages 65-74: 66.9% used the internet, **22.3% never have**, 93.8% go online by mobile phone, 20.2% use internet banking.
  - The article says older people rely on TV, newspapers and other offline sources.
  - The mobile-phone percentage is probably a share of internet users, not of everyone in the age group. Treat the denominator as unclear.
  - Source: [eCommerce Asocijacija Srbije, 2026-06-01, industry association](https://ecommserbia.org/2026/06/01/generacijske-razlike-u-koriscenju-elektronske-trgovine-uredaja-i-digitalnih-usluga)
- A search snippet from the same source family says 24.8% of internet users downloaded official forms from a public administration site or app in 2025. This was not verified on a fetched page and comes from the same ecommserbia article — [search result](https://ecommserbia.org/2026/06/01/generacijske-razlike-u-koriscenju-elektronske-trgovine-uredaja-i-digitalnih-usluga)
- RZS 2022: 83.2% of households had internet access and 95% had mobile phones. Sample: 2,800 households and individuals aged 16-74, by telephone interview. — [021.rs, 2022, media](https://www.021.rs/story/Info/Nauka-i-tehnologija/320562/RZS-Internet-prikljucak-u-Srbiji-ima-vise-od-83-odsto-domacinstava.html)
- Eurostat 2023: 33% of people in Serbia have at least basic digital skills, against 56% in the EU. Only Romania is lower (28%), and Montenegro is at 52%. 81% of Serbian internet users go online by mobile phone. — [N1, 2024, media citing Eurostat](https://n1info.rs/vesti/samo-trecina-stanovnika-srbije-ima-osnovne-digitalne-vestine/); [Eurostat news, 2024-02-22, official](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20240222-1)
- EU averages, not Serbia: in 2024, 70.0% of EU residents aged 16-74 used a public authority website or app. In 2025, 36% of EU residents aged 65-74 used eID, against 61% of those aged 35-44. — [Eurostat, 2025-02-26, official](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250226-1); [Eurostat E-government and eID, official](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=E-government_and_electronic_identification)
- Red Cross of Serbia baseline study, 2019 (outdated, but the only Serbian study focused on older people):
  - 43% of older participants did not use the internet.
  - 90% of those who did use it did not use any e-services.
  - Most were unaware that the eGovernment portal existed.
  - Nobody used "Moj doktor" or e-banking.
  - Barriers: not knowing about services, low confidence, income too low to buy devices, smartphones seen as too complex, and a preference for dealing with people in person.
  - Source: [Red Cross of Serbia / Institute of Social Sciences / UNFPA, 2019, NGO/IO](https://www.redcross.org.rs/en/resources/publications/ageing-and-digital-inclusion-baseline-study-with-recommendations/) ([PDF](https://www.redcross.org.rs/media/6183/starenje-i-digitalna-ukljucenost-web.pdf))

### Inferences
- The project's assumptions hold: phones are how people aged 65-74 get online (about 94% of those online), and about 22% of that group have never been online. The guide will mostly be used by a helper, such as a family member, volunteer or counter clerk, sitting with the older person. People aged 75 and over are probably even further behind, but no Serbian data covers them.
- Low basic digital skills (33%) next to high internet use (about 90%) means many users can open Viber or watch videos but cannot fill in a form. Steps such as "upload a photo of both sides of your ID card" and "make a password with a special character" are the likely failure points.

### Gaps
- Serbian e-government use broken down by age, education or income. The RZS PDF was not parsed, and the RZS database (data.stat.gov.rs) was not queried. A team member could pull the age tables directly from G202516020.pdf.
- People aged 75 and over: no data at all, because RZS and Eurostat cover only ages 16-74.
- Eurostat skills by age for Serbia (isoc_sk_dskl_i): not retrieved.
- Education and income breakdowns: none found in the sources read.

## 2. eUprava / eID accounts: how many, how people get them, and pain points

### Takeaway
eUprava accounts grew from 2 million (August 2023) to 2.2 million (June 2024), and a recent official statement says more than 3 million. Accounts are created three ways:
- online with a username and password, after uploading a scan of an ID document and waiting for manual activation within 48 hours,
- with a qualified certificate,
- at a registration counter: post offices, municipal offices, the Tax Administration or Mobi Banka.

The ConsentID app is used for 31% of logins (2024). The official FAQ has no phone help line, only a contact form. No Ombudsman or civil-society report on eID pain points for older people was found.

### Cited Findings
- 2 million registered eUprava users. The ministry says this saved citizens more than 170 million counter visits, and almost 3 million documents have been delivered to users' digital mailboxes. — [MIT, 2023-08-25, official](https://www.mit.gov.rs/vest/sr/2499/-portal-euprava-dostigao-je-dva-miliona-korisnika.php)
- An Ipsos survey for the Office for IT and eGovernment (ITE), run through the World Bank EDGE project, found:
  - 2.2 million citizens have accounts.
  - 86% were satisfied with the last e-service they used.
  - 60% prefer to deal with the administration online.
  - **31% log in with the ConsentID app**, more than double the year before.
  - The most-used service is booking an appointment for an ID card or passport.
  - The source gives no age breakdown.
  - Source: [021.rs, 2024-06-14, media reporting a government survey](https://www.021.rs/info/srbija/378594/vlada-srbije-deli-istrazivanje-skoro-svi-gradjani-zadovoljni-eupravom); [ITE news, official](https://www.ite.gov.rs/vest/sr/7797/rezultati-istrazivanja-pokazali-visok-nivo-zadovoljstva-korisnika-elektronskih-usluga.php)
- "More than 3 million users" and "more than 150 services", stated by ITE director Mihailo Jovanović. The fetched article showed no date, and the search index places it in 2026. — [Tanjug, undated (2026 per search index), media](https://www.tanjug.rs/ekonomija/srbija/284715/jovanovic-gradani-i-privreda-vise-od-150-usluga-drzave-mogu-da-koriste-elektronski/vest). Treat 3 million as unverified until a dated official source confirms it.
- Ways to register, from the eID portal FAQ:
  - username and password, for citizens and foreign nationals,
  - qualified electronic certificate,
  - at a registration counter in a post office, municipal office, local Tax Administration office or Mobi Banka.

  The ConsentID activation parameters or QR code are issued at those counters or generated with a qualified certificate. The counter list is at **https://euprava.gov.rs/eidlokacije**. The FAQ lists **no phone or email** for help, only a contact form, and says nothing about help for older people or assisted registration. — [eid.gov.rs FAQ, fetched 2026-10-07, official](https://eid.gov.rs/sr-Latn-RS/najcesca-pitanja-i-odgovori)
- Online username-and-password registration has five steps:
  1. Go to eid.gov.rs and open **Мој налог** (My account).
  2. Upload a scan or photo of both sides of the ID card or passport (at most 2 files, 3 MB each).
  3. Enter an email address, which becomes the username, and a password with at least 8 characters including an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, a digit and a special character.
  4. Open the confirmation link sent to the email within 24 hours (check spam).
  5. Wait for staff to activate the account within 48 hours.

  The page has a downloadable PDF guide, a contact form at eid.gov.rs/sr-Cyrl-RS/kontakt, and a "Читај ми" (read to me) audio feature. — [euprava.gov.rs registration page, fetched 2026-10-07, official](https://euprava.gov.rs/registracija-korisnico-ime-lozinka-rs); see also [Пријава мобилним телефоном (ConsentID)](https://euprava.gov.rs/prijava-eID)
- Anyone legally capable and over 16 can register, and Serbian citizens need a valid biometric ID card or passport. — [eid.gov.rs terms of use, official](https://eid.gov.rs/sr-Latn-RS/uslovi-koriscenja); [ITE eID page, official](https://www.ite.gov.rs/tekst/en/1140/electronic-identification-eid.php)
- Energy-vulnerable customer status (a discount on electricity and gas bills): pensioners whose pension is up to 31,092.11 RSD now get it without applying, because the local government starts the procedure itself. Other households apply to the local social-welfare office, not through eUprava. — [Kurir, media, date not checked](https://biznis.kurir.rs/novcanik/10110282/energetski-ugrozen-kupac-prijava-struja-i-grejanje); [Paragraf, Uredba o energetski ugroženom kupcu, legal database](https://www.paragraf.rs/propisi/uredba-o-energetski-ugrozenom-kupcu-republike-srbije.html). The figure may be out of date because the threshold is indexed.

### Inferences
- The guide's main paths match the official ones: online registration (hard for older people because it needs a scan, an email account and a complex password) and registration at a counter (easier, which justifies the post-office card).
- Mobi Banka and municipal offices also issue ConsentID activation. The card could say "пошта или општина" (post office or municipal office) instead of only the post office.
- The official channel offers no phone help, so the guide's "Нисам успео/ла" (I did not manage) branch has to send people to an in-person counter or a trusted helper, not to a hotline.

### Gaps
- No Zaštitnik građana (Ombudsman) report on eUprava and older people was found. The Ombudsman result that came up was Croatian and was left out.
- No Serbian media or civil-society account of eID registration problems was found. Searches for "ID za saglasnost penzioneri problemi" returned nothing relevant.
- No figures were found for ConsentID downloads, counter registrations, or users by age.
- Whether eUprava has a call centre number: none is listed on the eID FAQ. The team should check before stating there is none.
- Pension-related services and "moj eGrađanin": no source found.

## 3. Programmes already helping older or vulnerable people with digital services

### Takeaway
The most relevant active programme is the Red Cross of Serbia with UNFPA, under the Swiss-funded "PRO – Local Governance for People and Nature" programme. Its local Red Cross volunteers train older people in eUprava and other e-services in 6 municipalities; 257 people had been trained in Velika Plana alone by May 2025. The Red Cross also published a 2019 Cyrillic step-by-step guide to e-services, now outdated, which is the closest Serbian precedent for this project. NALED, UNDP and ITE ran "eUprava za sve" in 10 municipalities (July 2025 to June 2026), including citizen info days. UNDP's earlier digital literacy work in gerontology centres (2021) is older.

### Cited Findings
- UNFPA Serbia and the Red Cross of Serbia signed a memorandum with Velika Plana (municipality, Red Cross branch and social-welfare centre) on digital literacy for older people. It is part of "PRO – Local Governance for People and Nature" (UNOPS, UNICEF, UNFPA, UNEP), funded by the Government of Switzerland. Training covers eGovernment, eBanking, eHealth, Viber, WhatsApp, internet safety, appointment booking and e-cadastre. The 6 municipalities are Kragujevac, Kruševac, Velika Plana, Zaječar, Boljevac and Niš, and 257 people had been trained in Velika Plana. — [UNFPA Serbia, 2025-05-30, IO](https://serbia.unfpa.org/en/news/memorandum-understanding-signed-enhancing-digital-literacy-older-persons-velika-plana); [Red Cross of Serbia, IO/NGO](https://www.redcross.org.rs/en/news/velika-plana-is-the-third-municipality-to-sign-a-memorandum-of-cooperation/)
- The Red Cross of Serbia publication "Водич за коришћење е-услуга" (Step-by-Step Guide for Use of Selected E-Services), 2019, by N. Todorović, M. Vračević and others, with the Institute of Social Sciences and UNFPA. It is a Cyrillic PDF covering the eUprava portal, personal documents, appointments, medicine search, the courts portal and banking apps. **It is outdated (2019).** — [Red Cross of Serbia, 2019, NGO/IO](https://www.redcross.org.rs/en/resources/publications/step-by-step-guide-for-use-of-selected-e-services/)
- Red Cross of Serbia, "Inicijativa za socijalno uključivanje starijih osoba – uspesi i naučene lekcije" (social inclusion of older people: successes and lessons learned). — [PDF, date not checked, NGO](https://www.redcross.org.rs/media/5817/socijalna-inkluzija-srb.pdf)
- "eUprava za sve" (eGovernment for All), run by ITE with the Ministry of Public Administration and Local Self-Government, NALED and UNDP, July 2025 to June 2026:
  - Municipalities: Beočin, Bela Palanka, Čačak, Despotovac, Ćuprija, Kragujevac, Niš, Pirot, Veliko Gradište and Vranje.
  - Activities included citizen info days ("Dani i Karavan eUprave"), for example Kragujevac on 2026-03-16, Niš on 2026-03-18 and Čačak on 2026-03-23. **These events are over and the project ended in June 2026.**
  - NALED contact: +381 11 337 30 63, naled@naled.rs.
  - Sources: [NALED, 2025-10-23, NGO](https://naled.rs/vest-deset-gradova-i-opstina-nastavlja-digitalnu-transformaciju-kroz-projekat-euprava-za-sve-11335); [UNDP Serbia, IO](https://www.undp.org/serbia/news/egovernment-all-new-project-launched-advance-digital-capacity-local-governments); [Politika, media](https://www.politika.rs/sr/clanak/682863/euprava-za-sve-pokrenut-projekat-za-digitalni-napredak-lokalnih-samouprava)
- NALED's DIGIT project (Democratic Innovation for Governance, Inclusion and Transparency) has a special focus on older people, young people not in work or education, rural women and persons with disabilities. — [NALED, date not checked, NGO](https://naled.rs/en/projekti-digit-democratic-innovation-for-governance-inclusion-and-transparency-11626)
- "Digitalna ekspedicija" (Digital Expedition), a 16-city caravan in 2021 run by the trade and labour ministries, ITE and the New Literacy Programme (USAID, Propulsion, UNDP). It included a UNDP-supported pilot of digital education in **20 gerontology centres**. Topics were email, eUprava services, e-banking basics and internet safety. **Outdated (2021).** — [srbija.gov.rs, 2021-10-16, official](https://www.srbija.gov.rs/vest/en/179794/introducing-serbian-citizens-to-benefits-of-digital-space.php)
- UNDP Serbia's Country Programme Document for 2026-2030 has been published. Its digital-governance content was not reviewed. — [UNDP CPD 2026-2030, 2025-05/06, IO](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-06/2-undp-serbia-cpd-2026-2030-and-rrf_final-29-may-1.pdf)
- UNDG/UN story: "Digital Literacy and Skills Open Doors for Older Women in Serbia". — [UNDG, date not checked, IO](https://undg.org/node/135807)
- A UNDP story, "Digital skills for every stage of life", exists but returned HTTP 403 when fetched. — [UNDP Serbia](https://www.undp.org/serbia/stories/digital-skills-every-stage-life)
- Media guide "eUprava, kako napraviti nalog i koristiti usluge bez odlaska na šalter" (how to make an eUprava account and use services without going to a counter). — [cdi.rs, date not checked](https://cdi.rs/euprava-nalog/)

### Inferences
- The Red Cross network is the natural distribution partner. It already has older-person trainees, local volunteers and a UNFPA-backed eUprava curriculum in 6 municipalities. A free, phone-first, regularly updated guide would replace its 2019 PDF and would not compete with it.
- NALED and ITE have shown they want citizen-facing outreach ("eUprava za sve" info days) and have published contacts. They are the most plausible route to an informal official endorsement or a link from euprava.gov.rs.

### Gaps
- The search turned up no "Digitalni ambasadori" (digital ambassadors) programme.
- Not covered: Amity, SeConS, Belgrade Open School, library digital-literacy courses, Pošta Srbije initiatives, and gerontology-centre programmes after 2021.
- No ITE or MIT campaign aimed specifically at older people was found, and no official video guides were located. The registration page has an audio feature and PDF guides.

## 4. Official eUprava / eID help resources

### Takeaway
The official help consists of:
- the eid.gov.rs FAQ,
- the step-by-step registration page on euprava.gov.rs, which has a PDF guide and a "Читај ми" audio feature,
- the list of counter locations (euprava.gov.rs/eidlokacije),
- a contact form.

No phone help line is listed. Nothing in this help is aimed at older people or offers assisted registration.

### Cited Findings
- FAQ and help section: registration guides, login methods, troubleshooting, PDF manuals (password change, address change, moving to a new device) and a contact form. No phone or email is listed, and nothing covers help for older people. — [eid.gov.rs FAQ, official](https://eid.gov.rs/sr-Latn-RS/najcesca-pitanja-i-odgovori)
- Counter locations: **https://euprava.gov.rs/eidlokacije** (post offices, municipal offices, Tax Administration, Mobi Banka). — [eid.gov.rs FAQ, official](https://eid.gov.rs/sr-Latn-RS/najcesca-pitanja-i-odgovori)
- Registration page with a downloadable PDF guide, the "Читај ми" audio feature and the contact form at https://eid.gov.rs/sr-Cyrl-RS/kontakt. — [euprava.gov.rs, official](https://euprava.gov.rs/registracija-korisnico-ime-lozinka-rs)
- Portal eID description: a single place for identification and login. — [eid.gov.rs portal page, official](https://eid.gov.rs/sr-Latn-RS/portal-za-elektronsku-identifikaciju)
- The 2019 Red Cross guide (Section 3) is the only third-party Serbian guide for older people found.

### Inferences
- Quality: the official material is complete on procedure but written for literate, confident users. Its password rules, scan uploads and 24/48-hour waits are the points where an older user needs the extra explanation this guide provides.

### Gaps
- No official video guides (for example on YouTube) were found or checked.
- No call-centre number was found; this needs a manual check before the site makes any claim either way.

## 5. Funding: calls and small-grant programmes for 2025-2026

### Takeaway
No open call for 2026 aimed specifically at digital inclusion of older people was found. The most relevant routes are:
- the EU Civil Society Facility (CSF–IPA III) call for Serbia, about €4.1 million. Its reported deadline is **2026-10-10, three days away**, and it is unverified.

## 6. Civic tech and Balkan precedents for community-built e-government help

### Takeaway
The closest regional precedent is ICEDA (Increasing Civic Engagement in the Digital Agenda), funded by the EU, with CRTA as the Serbian partner. It set up volunteer-run "e-Government Support Centres" in 5 Western Balkan countries. In North Macedonia alone, more than 6,700 citizens had been helped by August 2021. It was a volunteer-and-stand model, not a software tool. No community-built eID helper that a government adopted or endorsed was found.

### Cited Findings
- Metamorphosis Foundation and ICEDA partners gave 15 sub-grants for e-government support centres to CSOs in Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia. The model: a trained pool of volunteers staffing help stands, social-media promotion and an interactive web page. — [Metamorphosis, 2021, NGO](https://metamorphosis.org.mk/en/from-citizens-to-citizens-through-iceda-e-government-support-centers-to-greater-use-of-public-e-services-and-tools/); [Metamorphosis, NGO](https://metamorphosis.org.mk/en/5-new-civil-society-organizations-from-the-western-balkans-join-the-iceda-regional-network/)
- CRTA (Serbia) is an ICEDA implementing partner. — [CRTA ToR, 2020, NGO](https://crta.rs/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/ToR-Increasing-Civic-Engagement-in-the-Digital-Agenda-WB-Report.pdf); [Metamorphosis, NGO](https://metamorphosis.org.mk/en/from-citizens-to-citizens-through-iceda-e-government-support-centers-to-greater-use-of-public-e-services-and-tools/)

## 7. Plain language, accessibility and script rules

### Takeaway
The Law on the Use of the Serbian Language in Public Life and Protection and Preservation of the Cyrillic Script (Official Gazette 89/2021) makes Cyrillic mandatory for public bodies and public companies; a private volunteer site is only encouraged to use it. The project's choice of Cyrillic first, with generated Latin pages, fits the law. A 2026 Caritas Serbia report finds that the digitalisation of public services has not come with communication accessibility. No Serbian plain-language law and no binding web-accessibility guideline for this kind of site were found.

### Cited Findings
- The law was adopted on 2021-09-15 (Official Gazette RS 89/2021). It sets obligations for public bodies and companies with majority public ownership, and incentives for private companies. — [Open Parliament, law text, official](https://otvoreniparlament.rs/uploads/akta/Zakon%20o%20upotrebi%20srpskog%20jezika%20u%20javnom%20%C5%BEivotu%20i%20za%C5%A1titi%20i%20o%C4%8Duvanju%20%C4%87iri%C5%A1kog%20pisma.pdf); [021.rs, 2021, media](https://www.021.rs/info/srbija/283766/usvojen-predlog-zakona-o-ocuvanju-cirilice-obaveze-za-javna-stimulacije-za-privatna-preduzeca)
- Caritas Serbia report on communication accessibility (2026-06). A search snippet says the Ombudsman and the Ministry for Human and Minority Rights found that the digitalisation of public services was not matched by digital and communication accessibility. It also says many public-authority websites fail basic standards: no alternative text, inaccessible forms, no sign-language content. The PDF could not be parsed, so this comes from the search snippet only. — [Caritas Serbia, 2026-06, NGO](https://caritas.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Izvestaj-Caritasa-Srbije-o-komunikacijskoj-pristupacnosti-u-Srbiji.pdf)
- The Law on the Official Use of Language and Script also applies to public bodies. — [SKGO copy, official](https://www.skgo.org/storage/app/media/uploaded-files/4-zakon-o-sluzbenoj-upotrebi-jezika-i-pisama.docx)

### Inferences
- The site already meets or exceeds what the law expects (Cyrillic source plus Latin). The project's alt-text rule directly addresses the main failure the Caritas report notes on government sites.

### Gaps
- Not located: "Smernice za izradu veb prezentacija organa državne uprave" (guidelines for state administration websites), its current version, and whether Serbia has transposed the EU Web Accessibility Directive or EN 301 549.
- No plain-language (jednostavan jezik / lako za čitanje) standard for Serbian was found.
